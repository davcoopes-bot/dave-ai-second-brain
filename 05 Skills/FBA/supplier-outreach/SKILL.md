# Skill: Supplier Outreach

> **Purpose:** Generate a sharp, specific first contact email to an Alibaba or direct supplier. Not a generic template — every email is built from the actual product specs and supplier context so it reads as a serious, informed buyer.
>
> **Why this matters:** Suppliers receive hundreds of copy-paste enquiry emails. A specific, well-structured message signals a real buyer and gets prioritised. Vague messages get ignored or get the minimum-effort response.

---

## Step 1 — Read the Product Context

Before asking Dave anything, read the relevant niche page for the product being sourced:

- For silicone splatter screen: `06 Wiki/wiki/fba/niche-silicone-splatter-screen.md`
- For cable management: `06 Wiki/wiki/fba/niche-cable-management.md`
- For any other niche: check `06 Wiki/system/index.md` → FBA section

Extract:
- Product non-negotiables (the specs that must be confirmed)
- Target sell price and COGS range
- Differentiation brief (what makes this product different from generic versions)
- Brand name (Voya)

Also check `03 Projects/FBA Research/(C) Alibaba Supplier Shortlist — [niche].md` if it exists — it may already have supplier-specific notes.

---

## Step 2 — Ask Four Questions

Ask Dave these four questions in one message:

1. **Supplier name and platform** — What's the supplier's name, and where did you find them? (Alibaba, Made-in-China, direct website)

2. **What caught your attention** — What specifically stood out about this supplier? (Their product listing, certifications, price, reviews — anything worth referencing in the email)

3. **Any concerns to probe** — Is there anything you want to specifically test or challenge in this email? (e.g. "their listing shows a foldable version — ask if they do solid one-piece", "unclear if they do full silicone handle wrap")

4. **Tone** — Straight business inquiry, or slightly warmer? (Default is professional and direct)

---

## Step 3 — Generate the Email

Build the email using the product context from Step 1 and Dave's answers from Step 2.

**Email structure:**

**Subject line:** `[Product name] — OEM Inquiry / Sample Request`
Keep it clear. Suppliers scan subject lines fast.

**Opening (1 sentence):** State who you are and what market you're sourcing for. No fluff.
> "I'm sourcing a [product] for the Australian and US Amazon FBA market under my own brand, Voya."

**Spec confirmation (3–5 bullet questions):** Lead with the non-negotiables from the niche page. Frame as questions, not demands. Only include specs that are genuinely uncertain — don't ask them to confirm things that are obvious from their listing.

**Order intention (1–2 sentences):** Give them a real number so they know it's worth their time.
> "Initial order: [X] units, scaling based on sell-through. OEM required — custom logo and packaging."

**Request (2–3 specific asks):**
- Unit pricing at [MOQ] / [2× MOQ] / [5× MOQ]
- Sample cost and estimated lead time
- Production lead time for first order
- Certifications (LFGB or FDA for food-contact products — always ask for kitchen items)

**Sign-off:** Name + brand name. No title needed at this stage.

---

## Email Quality Rules

- **Under 200 words** — shorter is better. Suppliers read fast. Long emails signal an amateur buyer.
- **Specific over generic** — reference the actual product variant, the actual spec concern, the actual size. Never write "high quality product" or "looking for a reliable supplier."
- **One email per supplier** — don't send the same email to multiple suppliers in one message. Each one gets its own tailored version based on what you saw in their listing.
- **No desperation signals** — don't say "we're just starting out" or "this is our first product." State your brand, state your specs, state your order intention. That's it.
- **No pricing negotiation in the first email** — get their standard pricing first, negotiate after samples are approved.

---

## Step 4 — Review and Output

Present the email to Dave with:

1. The full email (ready to copy-paste)
2. A one-line note on anything you couldn't personalise without more info — e.g. "I left the unit price blank at the 500-unit tier — fill in once you see their quote sheet"
3. Any red flags noticed in what Dave described about the supplier

Wait for Dave to approve or request changes before finalising.

## Notes

- **Voya** is the brand name for Dave and Josh's FBA operation. Use it consistently.
- Kitchen products sold on Amazon require food-grade certification — always ask for LFGB (EU standard, widely held by Chinese factories) or FDA. Don't skip this.
- If a supplier can't confirm the non-negotiables in the first exchange, don't chase them. Move to the next supplier on the shortlist.

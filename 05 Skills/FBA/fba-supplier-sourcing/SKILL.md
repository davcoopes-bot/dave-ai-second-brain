# FBA Supplier Sourcing — Full Workflow

Built for Dave and Josh. First product, launching from Australia, $60k to work with. Every section is executable — no theory padding.

---

## 1. Alibaba Search Strategy

### Search Terms
- Start with the **generic product name**, not a brand name (e.g. "stainless steel water bottle" not "HydroFlask style")
- Add a material or spec if you know it: "bamboo cutting board", "TPE yoga mat"
- Try 2–3 variants — manufacturers title things differently
- Avoid overly specific searches early; go broad, filter after

### Filters to Apply (in order)
1. **Supplier Type:** Manufacturer (not Trading Company — Trading Companies add margin and reduce control)
2. **Trade Assurance:** ON — protects your payment if goods don't match specs
3. **Gold Supplier:** ON — minimum quality signal; not a guarantee but filters the worst
4. **Minimum Order:** Set to match your budget (e.g. if you want to test at 200 units, filter accordingly)
5. **Verified Supplier:** ON if available — Alibaba has done a factory audit

### Reading Search Results
- Ignore the hero image — click through and read the listing
- Check transaction volume in the past 6 months — higher is better
- Note response time shown on the listing — anything over 24h is a yellow flag
- Look at the "Years on Alibaba" badge — 5+ years preferred for first order
- Scan the product range — a supplier making 200 unrelated products is likely a trading company

## 2. Supplier Vetting Checklist

Run every shortlisted supplier through this before contact.

| Criteria | Green | Yellow | Red |
|---|---|---|---|
| Years on Alibaba | 5+ | 2–4 | Under 2 |
| Verified/Audited | Yes | — | No |
| Response Rate | 90%+ | 75–89% | Under 75% |
| On-Time Delivery | 90%+ | 80–89% | Under 80% |
| Staff Count | 50+ for factory | 20–49 | Under 20 (likely trading co) |
| Exported to AU or US | Yes | Other Western markets | Only domestic |
| Has own factory | Yes (provides factory photos) | Unclear | Refuses to confirm |
| Sample availability | Yes, standard + custom | Standard only | No samples |
| English communication | Clear and responsive | Workable | Garbled / template-only |

**Extra checks:**
- Open the company profile page — look for factory photos, not just product shots
- Check their product range: does it make sense for a single manufacturer?
- Look at reviews if available — read the 3-star ones, not just the 5-stars
- Search their company name + "scam" or "review" on forums (Alibaba, Reddit, SourcingBro)

---

## 3. Shortlisting — From 20 to 3–5

After running vetting, cut using this hierarchy:

1. **Cut anyone who fails 2+ Red criteria** — no exceptions
2. **Cut trading companies** unless the product genuinely requires aggregation (rare for first product)
3. **Cut suppliers with no sample availability** — non-negotiable; you will not order blind
4. **Cut suppliers with response times over 48h** on initial inquiry — if they're slow now, they'll be slower later
5. **Rank remaining by:** export experience to AU/US > verified status > years on platform > response rate

Target: 3–5 suppliers to contact. More than 5 dilutes your focus. Fewer than 3 is too few to compare.

## 4. First Contact Message Template

Send this via Alibaba messaging. Do not use your personal email first — keep it on platform for Trade Assurance protection.

---

**Subject:** Inquiry — [PRODUCT NAME] — AU/US Market

Hi [SUPPLIER NAME] team,

My name is [YOUR NAME], and I'm the sourcing manager for [BRAND NAME], an e-commerce brand selling in Australia and the US.

We're currently evaluating manufacturers for [PRODUCT NAME] and your company has come up as a strong match. We're looking to place an initial order and scale volume with a reliable long-term partner.

Could you please provide the following:

1. **MOQ** — What is your minimum order quantity for this product?
2. **Unit Price** — Price at MOQ, and price at [2x MOQ] if available
3. **Sample availability** — Can you send a sample to Australia? What is the cost and lead time?
4. **Lead time** — Production lead time after order confirmation?
5. **Customisation** — Can you do custom packaging, logo, or colour variants?
6. **Certifications** — What safety/compliance certifications does this product hold? (e.g. CE, RoHS, ASTM, AS/NZS)
7. **Payment terms** — What are your standard terms?

We are serious buyers looking to move quickly. Looking forward to your response.

Regards,
[YOUR NAME]
[BRAND NAME]

---

**Notes on the template:**
- "Sourcing manager" is legitimate even if you're doing it yourself — it's a role, not a lie
- Don't mention Amazon, FBA, or that it's your first product
- Don't ask about everything at once beyond this list — it signals inexperience
- Send the same message to all 3–5 shortlisted suppliers and compare responses

## 5. MOQ Negotiation

### Realistic Starting MOQs by Product Type
| Product Type | Typical MOQ Range | Negotiable Target |
|---|---|---|
| Simple hardgoods (kitchen, fitness) | 200–500 units | 100–200 |
| Electronics / tech accessories | 500–1,000 units | 300–500 |
| Apparel / textile | 100–300 per SKU | 50–100 |
| Supplements / consumables | 500–1,000 units | 300–500 |
| Complex custom products | 1,000+ | 500–1,000 |

### How to Negotiate Down
1. **Lead with volume intent:** "We're starting at [lower MOQ] for the first order, but our plan is to scale to [2–3x] within 6 months as we validate the market."
2. **Anchor on the relationship:** "We're choosing one long-term manufacturing partner, not shopping around for the cheapest price. We want someone we grow with."
3. **Offer faster payment:** "If we can agree on [lower MOQ], we can do 50% upfront rather than 30%." — suppliers value cash flow.
4. **Accept a higher unit price in exchange for lower MOQ** — this is the standard trade-off and it's worth it for a first order.

### When to Push vs Accept
- **Push** if the quoted MOQ is more than 3x your target test quantity
- **Accept** if the MOQ is within 50% of your target and the supplier is otherwise excellent
- **Walk away** if they won't budge past a quantity you genuinely can't sell in 90 days — dead inventory is a cash trap

## 6. Sample Ordering Process

### Ordering
- Request samples from your top 2–3 suppliers simultaneously — don't wait for one before ordering the next
- Typical sample cost: $30–$150 USD per sample + shipping ($30–$80 DHL/FedEx to AU)
- Shipping time to Australia: 5–10 business days via express courier
- Pay via Alibaba Trade Assurance or PayPal — never wire transfer for samples
- Request a production-spec sample, not a showroom sample if possible

### Sample Inspection Checklist

**Dimensions & Weight**
- [ ] Matches spec sheet dimensions (measure it)
- [ ] Weight matches — heavier = more FBA fees
- [ ] Fits within intended Amazon size tier

**Build Quality**
- [ ] No visible defects, seams, bubbles, or rough edges
- [ ] Materials match what was quoted
- [ ] Joins and fasteners are solid — stress test them
- [ ] Finish (paint, coating, texture) is consistent

**Functionality**
- [ ] Works as intended — test it properly, not just unboxing
- [ ] Test edge cases and failure points

**Packaging**
- [ ] Packaging is adequate for sea freight (can it survive a drop test?)
- [ ] FNSKU barcode placement will be viable
- [ ] Packaging matches what was quoted

**Compliance**
- [ ] Request the actual certification documents, not just a label
- [ ] Verify certification numbers if possible (most have online lookup)

**Branding (if applicable)**
- [ ] Logo is sharp, correct colours, correct placement
- [ ] No spelling errors on packaging (happens more than you'd think)

### Comparing Multiple Samples
Score each supplier 1–5 across: quality, packaging, branding accuracy, responsiveness during process. The supplier that communicates well during sampling will communicate well during production — weight this heavily.

### Accept vs Reject
- **Accept:** Minor cosmetic issues correctable in spec sheet, functionality is solid, supplier is responsive
- **Reject:** Functionality failures, materials don't match quote, certifications can't be verified, supplier was slow or evasive during sampling

## 7. Landed Cost Calculator

### Formula

| Cost Component | Notes |
|---|---|
| Unit COGS | Alibaba unit price at your order quantity |
| Sea freight (per unit) | Total sea freight ÷ units ordered |
| Import duty (per unit) | Check AU tariff rate at abf.gov.au — varies by HS code |
| Customs broker fee (per unit) | ~$150–300 total ÷ units (use a broker for your first order) |
| Amazon inbound shipping (per unit) | Varies by weight/size — use Amazon's fee calculator |
| FBA fulfilment fee (per unit) | Use Amazon FBA Revenue Calculator at sellercentral |
| FBA storage (monthly per unit) | Budget for 60–90 days stock on hand |
| **Total Landed Cost** | **Sum of all above** |

### Worked Example (Hypothetical)

Assume: 300 units of a fitness product, $8 USD unit cost, 500g, standard size tier, selling for $45 AUD.

| Item | Per Unit (AUD approx) |
|---|---|
| Unit COGS ($8 USD) | $12.50 |
| Sea freight (est. $600 total) | $2.00 |
| Import duty (5% of COGS + freight) | $0.73 |
| Customs broker ($200 total) | $0.67 |
| Amazon inbound | $1.50 |
| FBA fulfilment fee | $5.00 |
| FBA storage (est.) | $0.50 |
| **Total Landed Cost** | **~$22.90** |
| Selling Price | $45.00 |
| Gross Margin before PPC | ~49% |

### Target Benchmark
- **COGS should be under 25% of selling price** to leave room for PPC (15–25%), returns (2–5%), and still hit 20%+ net margin
- If landed cost is above 40% of selling price, the numbers don't work — re-negotiate, find a cheaper supplier, or reprice

### Sea vs Air Freight
| | Sea | Air |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Low (~$1–3/unit for standard products) | High (~$5–15/unit) |
| Transit time | 25–40 days (China to AU) | 5–10 days |
| Use when | Initial stock, reorders, anything over 100kg | Urgent restocks, very light/time-sensitive products |
| Rule of thumb | Most products over 50kg total → sea wins | Under 50kg total shipment → run the numbers |

## 8. Red Flags to Watch

Treat these as automatic disqualifiers or immediate escalation triggers:

- **Ghosting after initial response** — they replied once, now silent. Move on; this is a preview of production.
- **Price bait-and-switch** — quoted $5/unit, now it's $7 "because of material costs." Lock pricing in writing before ordering.
- **Vague certifications** — "We have CE" with no document number or verifiable record. Ask for the actual cert PDF.
- **Pushing air freight without prompting** — some suppliers make margin on freight. If they're steering you to air for a large order, ask why.
- **Refusing sample customisation** — if they won't put your logo on a sample, they won't manage branding well on the full run.
- **Safety documentation in broken English** — indicates it was never properly audited. Compliance docs should be professional.
- **Factory photos that look like stock images** — reverse image search them.
- **Pressure to move off Alibaba** — "Let's use WeChat, I'll give you a better price." You lose Trade Assurance. Don't do it until you have an established relationship over multiple orders.
- **Payment only via wire transfer for samples** — established suppliers accept Alibaba Trade Assurance or PayPal for samples.

## 9. Timeline Expectations

Set expectations before you start — this is a long game and delays compound.

| Phase | Realistic Timeline |
|---|---|
| Alibaba research and shortlisting | 3–5 days |
| First contact to supplier response | 1–3 days |
| Negotiation and sample order placed | 3–7 days |
| Sample production | 5–15 days |
| Sample transit to Australia | 5–10 days (express courier) |
| Sample inspection and supplier decision | 3–7 days |
| **First contact → sample decision total** | **~4–6 weeks** |
| Order confirmed to production complete | 3–5 weeks |
| Sea freight China → Australia | 4–6 weeks |
| Customs clearance | 3–7 days |
| Amazon inbound processing | 3–5 days |
| **Order placed → live on Amazon total** | **~12–16 weeks** |

**Takeaway:** Place your first order the moment you confirm your sample. Every week of delay is a week your launch slips. Plan your first reorder before you're halfway through your initial stock — sea freight does not care about your stockout date.

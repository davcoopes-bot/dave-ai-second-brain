# FBA Listing Optimization — Full Workflow

For Dave and Josh building their first private label product. The goal is a listing that converts, not one that looks like every other generic Chinese copy-paste job.

---

## Phase 1 — Keyword Foundation

Build the keyword list before writing a single word of copy. Listing without keywords is guessing.

### Sources to pull from

| Source | Tool | What you're after |
|---|---|---|
| Seed keywords | Helium 10 Magnet | High-volume terms for your main product + 20-30 variations |
| Competitor reverse-ASIN | Helium 10 Cerebro | Keywords your top 3-5 competitors rank for organically |
| Listing mining | Manual scan | Exact phrases repeated across multiple top-10 listings |
| Review language | Manual scan | Words buyers actually use — often different from seller copy |

### Categorise every keyword

**Primary (Title)** — Highest search volume, most commercially relevant. Usually 1-3 terms. This is what you lead with.
- Volume threshold: 5,000+ monthly searches (US marketplace)

**Secondary (Bullets + Description)** — Mid-volume, high-intent variations. Capture the "almost primary" terms.
- Volume threshold: 500–5,000 monthly searches

**Long-tail (Backend)** — Specific, lower-volume, high-converting phrases. Misspellings, multi-word queries, niche use cases.
- Volume threshold: under 500, or any term not covered above

### Keyword priority logic

1. Relevance beats volume — a 10k search term that isn't your product is useless
2. Organic rank opportunity matters — check competition score in Helium 10, don't chase terms where top 10 are all huge brands
3. Buyer-intent signals: "for X", "best X for Y", "X that Z" — these convert better than generic nouns

## Phase 2 — Title Formula

### Structure

```
[Brand Name] + [Primary Keyword] + [Key Feature/Benefit] + [Secondary Keyword] + [Size/Quantity/Variant]
```

### Rules

- Max 200 characters — aim for 150-170 (mobile truncates around 80 chars; front-load ruthlessly)
- Front-load the primary keyword — don't waste the first 5 words on brand name if it's unknown
- No ALL CAPS words
- No promotional claims: "best", "amazing", "number one", "#1"
- No pricing, no "sale", no "free shipping"
- Use hyphens or commas to separate distinct phrases — improves readability and scan-ability
- Include a secondary keyword naturally — don't force it

### Before / After Example

**Weak title:**
> Amazing Premium Yoga Mat by FitLife — Super Comfortable Non-Slip Mat for Exercise, Great for Beginners, Professionals and All People Who Love Yoga

Problems: "Amazing", "Premium", "Super Comfortable" are claims not features. "All People Who Love Yoga" is wasted characters. Primary keyword buried. Zero specifics.

**Strong title:**
> FitLife Yoga Mat 6mm — Non-Slip Thick Exercise Mat with Carry Strap, Ideal for Hot Yoga, Pilates and Home Gym Workouts, 72" x 24"

Why it works: Primary keyword ("Yoga Mat") in position 2. Spec ("6mm") is a real differentiator. Secondary keywords ("Hot Yoga", "Pilates", "Home Gym") are discrete search terms. Size variant included. No fluff.

## Phase 3 — Bullet Points (5 Bullets)

### Structure for each bullet

```
BENEFIT IN CAPS (3-5 words) — Feature explanation that supports the benefit, includes a secondary keyword naturally, speaks to a specific use case or pain point.
```

Max 500 characters per bullet. Every bullet should do one job — don't cram two benefits into one.

### Bullet assignment

**Bullet 1 — Hero Benefit**
Address the #1 complaint in your niche's reviews. Pull this from review analysis on competitors. If the category's biggest complaint is "slides around during use", your first bullet solves that.

**Bullet 2 — Key Differentiator**
What do you have that the generic competition doesn't? Specific material, thickness, certification, unique design feature, better dimensions. Be concrete — not "high quality", but "6mm TPE foam rated for 10,000+ compression cycles".

**Bullet 3 — Secondary Feature + Use Case**
Expand the use case universe. Who else buys this? When? Where? This is where secondary keywords land naturally.

**Bullet 4 — Compatibility / What's Included / Who It's For**
Answer the "will this work for me?" question before the customer asks it. List compatibility, included accessories, sizing info, or ideal user profile. Reduces returns and increases confidence.

**Bullet 5 — Guarantee / Quality Assurance / Brand Promise**
Reduce perceived risk. Return policy, warranty, what happens if it breaks. This is the last thing they read before deciding — make them feel safe buying from a brand they've never heard of.

### What to avoid

- Keyword stuffing: "yoga mat exercise mat workout mat fitness mat gym mat" — looks spammy, reads like a bot
- Starting every bullet with "This product..." — lazy and wastes characters
- Vague adjectives: "high quality", "durable", "great" — say what specifically makes it so
- Repeating the title keyword in every bullet — use variations instead

## Phase 4 — Product Description / A+ Content

### Without Brand Registry (standard description)

Write 150-300 words. This is your chance to tell the story the bullets couldn't. Structure:

1. **Opening hook** — one sentence that calls out the target buyer and their problem
2. **How your product solves it** — specific, concrete, no fluff
3. **The experience of owning it** — paint the picture of life with the product
4. **Differentiation** — why yours vs. the sea of identical alternatives
5. **Call to action** — "Add to cart" or "Click Add to Cart above" (Amazon allows this)

Use line breaks. Walls of text don't get read. HTML tags `<br>` and `<b>` are supported in standard descriptions.

### With Brand Registry (A+ Content)

Build out these modules in this order:

| Module | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Hero image + headline | Brand statement, not product features — make it feel like a real brand |
| Feature icon row | 4-6 icons with short labels — the "at a glance" version of bullets |
| Lifestyle image + text | Show the product being used in context; text addresses a specific use case |
| Comparison chart | Your product vs. your own variants (or vs. generic alternatives) — never name competitors directly |
| Packaging / unboxing shot | Reduces "not as described" returns; builds trust |

A+ content does not directly affect search rank but measurably improves CVR. Prioritise after Brand Registry is live.

## Phase 5 — Main Image Brief

The main image is the single most important conversion lever on Amazon. It determines whether anyone clicks.

### Hero image requirements (non-negotiable)

- Pure white background (RGB 255, 255, 255) — Amazon requires this
- Product fills at least 85% of the frame
- No text, no logos, no lifestyle props, no borders
- Highest possible resolution — minimum 1,000px on the shortest side (zoom capability requires 1,600px+)
- Show the product at its most visually impressive angle

### Secondary image brief (build these in order)

1. **Lifestyle in use** — real person, real environment, using the product correctly
2. **Size/scale reference** — product next to a hand, common object, or with dimensions overlaid
3. **Feature callout graphic** — clean flat lay with arrows or callout lines pointing to key features
4. **Comparison chart image** — your product vs. "standard" — highlight your advantages visually
5. **Packaging shot** — shows what arrives at the door; builds unboxing confidence
6. **Infographic with key specs** — for technical buyers who want the numbers before reading

Do not finalize images last. Images take longest to brief, shoot, and edit. Brief the images after the title is locked, run them in parallel with bullet writing.

## Phase 6 — Backend Keywords

### The 250-byte field

Amazon gives you 250 bytes (roughly 250 characters) of backend keywords. These are invisible to shoppers but indexed by A9. Use every byte.

### Rules

- No commas — space-separated only
- No words already in your title or bullets — Amazon indexes those already; repetition wastes space
- No competitor brand names — policy violation, can get listing suppressed
- No claims you can't substantiate ("organic", "FDA approved" without certification)
- No ASINs

### What to include

- Misspellings of your primary keyword (people typo constantly)
- Synonyms and alternative names for the product
- Complementary search terms ("accessories for X", "X replacement", "X gift")
- Spanish translations of key terms (US marketplace has significant Spanish-language search volume)
- Niche use cases not mentioned in bullets
- Abbreviations and colloquial names

### Example (yoga mat)

```
exercise mat stretching mat pilates mat meditation mat anti-slip mat alfombra yoga estera ejercicio thick mat tpe mat non toxic mat eco mat gym mat floor mat workout mat beginners mat travel mat foldable mat carry bag included extra wide mat wide yoga mat
```

## Phase 7 — Competitor Listing Audit

Run this before writing your own listing and again before launch.

### Scoring framework

Score each competitor listing across 5 dimensions. Rate Strong / Average / Weak.

| Dimension | What to assess | Signal to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Title quality | Primary keyword placement, character use, specificity | Is the keyword in the first 5 words? Any wasted words? |
| Image quality | Main image impact, lifestyle quality, number of images | Would you click this on mobile? Are all 7 image slots used? |
| Bullet benefit-lead ratio | % of bullets that lead with a benefit vs. a feature | Benefit-led = buyer-first. Feature-led = lazy copy |
| Review sentiment match | Does listing copy address the top complaints in reviews? | Check 1-3 star reviews for pain points. Does the listing pre-empt them? |
| A+ presence | Does competitor have A+ content? Quality? | No A+ = gap you can exploit. Poor A+ = easy to beat |

### Output

Identify the 2-3 specific gaps your listing will exploit. Be concrete:

- "Their bullet 1 leads with a feature, not a benefit — ours leads with the exact complaint in their 2-star reviews"
- "No A+ content across all top 5 — we'll have it within 30 days of Brand Registry"
- "Main image is cluttered and props-heavy — ours is clean product-only and will dominate on mobile"

## Phase 8 — Conversion Rate Benchmarks

Know your numbers. These are goalposts, not guarantees.

| Metric | Weak | Average | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Organic CVR | under 5% | 5-10% | 10-15%+ |
| PPC CVR | under 4% | 4-8% | 8-12%+ |
| CTR (PPC) | under 0.3% | 0.3-0.5% | 0.5%+ |
| Sessions to first review | — | — | aim for 1 review per 25-50 sessions early |

### Diagnosing the problem

**Traffic problem** (not a listing problem):
- High CVR but low sessions
- Fix: keyword research, PPC bids, external traffic

**Listing problem** (not a traffic problem):
- High sessions, low CVR
- Fix: images first (biggest lever), then price check, then bullets

**Both problems:**
- Low sessions AND low CVR — common on launch
- Fix: images and price first, then PPC to drive volume, then iterate

If CVR is under 5% after 200+ sessions, stop spending on traffic and fix the listing. Throwing PPC at a broken listing is burning money.

## Phase 9 — Listing Launch Sequence

Do this in order. Skipping steps creates rework.

```
1. Keyword research         → Build master keyword list, categorise into primary/secondary/long-tail
2. Competitor audit         → Score top 5 competitors, identify your 2-3 gaps to exploit
3. Title                    → Lock title first. Everything else flows from it.
4. Bullets                  → Write all 5. Review for keyword coverage and benefit-lead ratio.
5. Backend keywords         → Fill 250 bytes with what's not already in title/bullets
6. Image brief              → Write the brief for photographer/designer. Don't wait until copy is done.
7. Description              → Write standard description or plan A+ module structure
8. Images delivered         → Review against brief before uploading
9. Listing goes live        → All 7 image slots filled, all copy complete before launch
10. A+ Content              → Build after Brand Registry approval (can take 3-8 weeks)
```

### Why images aren't last

Images take the longest to produce (brief to shoot to edit to revise = 1-3 weeks minimum). If you write copy first and brief images last, you're adding 2 weeks to your launch timeline for no reason. Brief images the day you lock the title. Run them in parallel.

## Quick Reference Checklist

Before submitting any listing, verify:

- [ ] Title: primary keyword in first 5 words, 150-170 chars, no banned terms
- [ ] All 5 bullets written, each under 500 chars, benefit-led
- [ ] Backend keywords: 250 bytes used, no repeats from title/bullets, no commas
- [ ] Main image: white background, product 85%+ of frame, no text
- [ ] Minimum 5 images uploaded (7 strongly preferred)
- [ ] Description or A+ content complete
- [ ] Competitor audit done — 2-3 gaps documented and addressed
- [ ] CVR benchmark noted — know what "good" looks like for your category

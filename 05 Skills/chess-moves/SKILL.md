# Skill: Chess Moves — Strategic Thinking Session

A Chess Moves session is not a brainstorm. It's a structured session to reach a clear position on a real decision. Dave comes in with a dilemma or question. He leaves with a position and the reasoning behind it — written down.

---

## Before Starting

Read these files:
1. `CLAUDE.md` — goals, current position, known risks
2. `GOALS.md` — milestones, income stream pipeline, strategy
3. `02 Chess Moves (Long-Term Planning)/(C) Chess Moves — Dave 2026.md` — existing settled positions, the big unsettled questions, income stream status board

If the decision involves FBA or trading, also check the relevant wiki pages via `06 Wiki/system/index.md`.

---

## Phase 1: Frame the Decision

Ask Dave:
- **What's the decision you're trying to make?** Be specific — "should I go hard on FBA or trading first" is a decision. "What should I do with my life" is not.
- **What are the options on the table?** List them out — don't assume.
- **What's driving the urgency?** Why are you thinking about this now?
- **What would a wrong decision cost you?** Time, money, opportunity.

Summarise what you heard back to Dave so he can correct anything before you start analysing.

---

## Phase 2: Stress-Test the Options

For each option on the table, challenge it hard. Cover:

**Upside case:** What does this look like if it works? Be specific — revenue numbers, timelines, what "working" actually means.

**Downside case:** What's the realistic bad outcome? What breaks? What does Dave lose?

**Dave's optimism filter:** Dave runs hot on optimism. For every upside, ask: "What would need to be true for this to actually happen? Is that realistic?"

**Opportunity cost:** If Dave goes hard on Option A, what does he not do? Is what he's giving up worth less than what he'd gain?

**Current evidence:** What does Dave actually know vs. what is he assuming? Flag every assumption clearly.

---

## Phase 3: Apply the Prime Directive

The prime directive is: **What is the single highest-leverage move Dave can make right now?**

Apply these filters:
1. Which option closes the gap to $10k/month fastest?
2. Which option uses Dave's existing advantages (trade income as runway, Josh as partner, $60k liquid)?
3. Which option is most at risk from Dave spreading too thin?
4. Which option, if it fails, leaves Dave in the best position?

---

## Phase 4: State the Position

After analysis, state a clear recommendation. No hedging. No "it depends." Dave can decide to disagree — but give him a position to push against.

Format:
```
**Recommended position:** [1–2 sentences — direct call]

**The core reasoning:** [3–5 sentences — why this wins over the alternatives]

**The key risk:** [What would need to happen for this to be wrong]

**The condition that would change this:** [If X happens, switch to Y]
```

---

## Phase 5: Update the Chess Moves File

After Dave agrees with a position (or modifies it to something he agrees with), update `02 Chess Moves (Long-Term Planning)/(C) Chess Moves — Dave 2026.md`:

1. Move the question from **Big Unsettled Questions** to **Settled Positions** (with date and reasoning)
2. Update the **Income Stream Status Board** if relevant
3. Update the **Last run** date at the top
4. Append a row to the **Chess Moves Sessions** table

Do not rewrite the file. Make targeted edits to the specific sections.

---

## Rules for This Skill

- **No vague conclusions.** Every session ends with a clear position written down.
- **Challenge optimism.** Dave's biggest blind spot is over-estimating upside. Push back on every rosy assumption.
- **One decision at a time.** If Dave shows up with three decisions, pick the most important one and focus there.
- **Don't recommend inaction.** "Wait and see" is only valid if there's a specific trigger to watch for. Define it.

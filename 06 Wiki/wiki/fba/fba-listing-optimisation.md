# Amazon FBA — Listing Optimisation

> **Category:** fba
> **Last updated:** 2026-05-03
> **Sources:** [[wiki/sources/src — Amazon Product Detail Page Rules.md]]

---

## Overview

Your listing is the only sales tool you have on Amazon. It determines whether customers find you (ranking), click on you (CTR), and buy from you (conversion). Every field — title, bullets, description, images, backend keywords — is both a ranking input and a conversion input. Getting this right before launch is non-negotiable: a weak listing wastes PPC spend and kills your honeymoon period.

---

## Title Rules & Best Practices

### Amazon's Hard Rules (violations = suppression)
- **Maximum 200 characters** including spaces — hard limit
- **Recommend ≤80 characters** — longer titles are truncated on mobile (majority of Amazon traffic)
- **Title Case only** — capitalise first letter of each word; no ALL CAPS
- **No emojis or symbols** at the beginning of each word
- **No HTML or code**
- **Prohibited content in titles:** prices, availability, promotional phrases ("best seller", "sale"), URLs, phone numbers, review requests, watermarks

### Title Structure Best Practice (not from policy — standard industry practice)
Build titles in this order:
```
[Brand] + [Primary Keyword] + [Key Feature/Differentiator] + [Size/Quantity/Colour if relevant]
```
Example for splatter screen:
```
Voya Silicone Splatter Screen for Frying Pans — Heat-Resistant Handle, Fine Mesh, 13 Inch
```

- Lead with your **most-searched keyword** (Amazon indexes title heavily)
- Include the **2–3 most important search terms** without keyword stuffing
- Keep it under 80 characters for mobile — everything after gets cut off in search results

## Bullet Points — Rules & Best Practices

### Amazon's Hard Rules
- No promotional phrases, prices, availability claims, or URLs
- Title Case — no ALL CAPS bullet headers unless it's a heading style
- No HTML (exception: `</br>` line breaks in description only, not bullets)
- Must accurately describe the product — no misleading claims

### Bullet Point Best Practice
You get 5 bullets. Use them in this order:
1. **Primary benefit / hero feature** — the #1 reason to buy
2. **Key differentiator** — what makes yours better than competitors'
3. **Material/build quality** — what it's made of, why that matters
4. **Use case / compatibility** — who it's for, what it fits
5. **What's in the box / guarantee / brand promise** — reduce purchase anxiety

Each bullet should be 1–2 sentences. Lead with the benefit, back it up with the feature:
```
✅ STAYS COOL WHILE COOKING — The silicone handle stays heat-resistant up to 450°F, so you can adjust the screen mid-cook without grabbing a cloth
❌ SILICONE HANDLE — Our handle is made from silicone
```

## Product Description

### Amazon's Hard Rules
- No HTML (line breaks `</br>` are the only exception)
- No review quotes or testimonials
- No time-sensitive information
- Must accurately describe the product

### Best Practice
If you are not Brand Registered: description sits below the fold and has low impact. Prioritise title, bullets, and images first.

If Brand Registered (Voya): description is replaced by **A+ Content** — use it. A+ Content:
- Increases conversion rate by ~5–15% on average
- Allows comparison charts, lifestyle images, brand story modules
- Suppresses competitor ads on your listing

## Images — Rules & Best Practices

### Amazon's Hard Rules (from policy — detailed image requirements at G1881)
- **No watermarks, text overlays, or logos on images** (violates policy)
- **No promotional badges** ("Best Seller", "Sale", etc.)
- **No URLs, phone numbers, or contact info** in images
- Main image must show the **actual product** (no props, no lifestyle shots as main)
- Main image requires **pure white background**

### Best Practice Image Sequence
| Slot | Content |
|------|---------|
| Main | Product on white background — clean, fills 85%+ of frame |
| 2 | Lifestyle — product in use (real kitchen, real context) |
| 3 | Feature callout — annotated image highlighting key differentiators |
| 4 | Size/scale reference — show dimensions clearly |
| 5 | Comparison chart — you vs. generic competitors |
| 6 | Infographic — materials, features, what's in the box |
| 7 | Lifestyle — second use case or emotional context |

**The main image drives CTR.** If your main image doesn't outperform competitors in the SERP, no amount of good copy saves you. Test it.

## Backend Keywords (Search Terms)

Not covered in the policy source — standard Amazon practice:
- 250 bytes maximum (not characters — some special characters count as 2 bytes)
- No need to repeat words already in title or bullets (Amazon indexes those automatically)
- No competitor brand names (policy violation)
- No repeated words — every word should appear once
- Include common misspellings, synonyms, alternate names

---

## Actionable Takeaways

1. **Write the title last** — do your keyword research first, then build the title around what people actually search
2. **80 characters max for mobile** — check how your title looks on a phone before publishing
3. **Lead every bullet with the benefit** — feature-first bullets kill conversion
4. **Main image is your most important asset** — it determines click-through before the customer reads a word
5. **Register the Voya brand** before launching — A+ Content and Brand Ads are significant competitive advantages
6. **Never stuff keywords** into titles or bullets — Amazon penalises keyword spam and it reads badly to humans

---

## Mistakes to Avoid

- Using ALL CAPS anywhere in titles or bullets — instant suppression risk
- Putting your price or "free shipping" in listing content — prohibited
- Copying a competitor's title structure without checking character count
- Launching with only 3–4 images when you could have 7
- Writing bullets that describe features instead of benefits
- Creating a new listing when a variation (size/colour) should be added to an existing parent ASIN instead
- Creating a duplicate listing for a product already in Amazon's catalogue

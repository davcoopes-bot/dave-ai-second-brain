# Amazon FBA — Ranking Factors

> **Category:** fba
> **Last updated:** 2026-05-03

---

## Overview

Amazon's search algorithm (commonly called A9 / A10) ranks products to maximise Amazon's revenue per search. It doesn't reward the best product — it rewards the product most likely to generate a sale. Every ranking input is either a **relevance signal** (does this product match what the customer searched?) or a **performance signal** (does this product actually get purchased?). Strong sellers optimise for both.

Note: Amazon does not publicly document its algorithm. This page synthesises known signals from the official policy document plus widely validated seller community knowledge.

---

## Key Ranking Factors

### 1. Sales Velocity
**The single most important signal.** Amazon ranks products that sell. More sales → higher rank → more sales. This is why launch strategy (honeymoon period, initial velocity) matters so much — you're trying to earn rank, not just make sales.

- Sales velocity is tracked by keyword — ranking on "silicone splatter screen" requires sales from searches for "silicone splatter screen"
- PPC helps here because a sponsored sale still counts as a sale for ranking purposes
- BSR (Best Seller Rank) is a proxy but not the same as keyword rank

### 2. Click-Through Rate (CTR)
If customers search a keyword and skip your listing, Amazon interprets this as "this product is less relevant." Low CTR = ranking drop over time.

CTR is driven by:
- Main image (most impactful — visible before the customer reads anything)
- Title (the snippet customers read in the SERP)
- Price (displayed in search results)
- Review count and star rating (visible in search results)

### 3. Conversion Rate (CVR)
Of all customers who click your listing, what percentage buy? High conversion = strong relevance signal to Amazon.

CVR is driven by:
- Quality of images (especially lifestyle and feature shots)
- Bullet point clarity and benefit communication
- Review quantity and quality
- Price vs. competitor price
- A+ Content (Brand Registry)
- Product accurately matching search intent (if they leave immediately, that's a negative signal)

### 4. Keyword Relevance
Amazon needs to understand what your product is before it can rank it. Keywords in the following fields are indexed and weighted (roughly in this order):

| Field | Weight |
|-------|--------|
| Product title | Highest |
| Backend search terms | High |
| Bullet points | Medium |
| Product description / A+ Content | Lower |
| Subject matter (backend) | Medium |

**From the official policy:** Titles must help customers "understand the product" — keyword stuffing that hurts readability will also hurt ranking over time (Amazon penalises listings with high bounce rates).

### 5. Review Velocity & Rating
- Review count affects both CTR (social proof in search results) and conversion
- Rating below 4.0 significantly hurts conversion — aim for 4.3+
- New reviews being added consistently signal an active, legitimate listing
- Amazon Vine can seed initial reviews legally

### 6. FBA Status
FBA listings rank higher than FBM (Fulfilled by Merchant) listings, all else equal, because Amazon controls the fulfilment experience and can guarantee Prime delivery. For Voya: always FBA.

### 7. Availability & In-Stock Rate
Going out of stock resets your ranking. Amazon learns that your listing can't be trusted to fulfil — and drops it. Running out of stock is one of the most damaging things you can do to a product's BSR and keyword rank.

### 8. Price Competitiveness
Amazon tracks whether your price is competitive relative to other sellers and other platforms (price parity policy). Pricing too high vs. competitors reduces your chance of winning the Buy Box and suppresses rank.

### 9. Brand Registry Signals (indirect)
Brand Registered sellers can use A+ Content, Sponsored Brand Ads, and Brand Store — all of which drive more sales and conversions, feeding back into ranking. The policy document notes that Brand Registry affects how listing contributions are weighted.

---

## Ranking Signals NOT In Your Control

- Competitor PPC spend (they can outbid you on your own keywords)
- Seasonal demand shifts
- Amazon's algorithmic changes (they update the algorithm regularly)
- Review manipulation by competitors (review bombing — report to Amazon)

---

## Actionable Takeaways

1. **Win the launch** — your honeymoon period (first 2–4 weeks) carries outsized ranking weight. Plan velocity targets before you launch, not after.
2. **Fix CTR before anything else** — if your click-through is low, fixing images and price is more valuable than any other optimisation.
3. **Never go out of stock** — maintain 60–90 days of inventory, especially in your first year. Stock-outs kill ranking and are hard to recover from.
4. **Target long-tail keywords first** — rank on lower-competition terms, generate sales, build authority, then move to head terms.
5. **Reviews are a moat** — every competitor starting after you has to climb the same review hill. Build reviews fast and legitimately (Vine, insert cards, follow-up sequences if allowed).

---

## Mistakes to Avoid

- Spending all PPC budget on head terms before you have reviews to convert the traffic
- Ignoring CTR — a beautiful listing with a weak main image will rank poorly
- Going out of stock at any point in your first 6 months
- Launching with 10–20 reviews and expecting to rank for competitive keywords
- Using keyword-stuffed titles that get penalised for poor UX metrics

## One-Line Summary

Positioning determines who your brand is for, what it stands for, and why someone should choose you — get it wrong and all logo and packaging work is wasted.

## Key Takeaways

### The "Logic of the Only" — The Core Framework

Before building anything, complete this sentence:

> **"We are the ONLY [category] that [unique action] for [specific audience] so they can [specific win]."**

If you can't complete it clearly, you have a product — not a brand. Products compete on price. Brands don't have to.

**Weak vs Strong:**
- ❌ "We sell resistance bands for home workouts"
- ✅ "We are the only home fitness brand that designs equipment specifically for apartment dwellers with no floor space, so they can train seriously without needing a gym"

### Finding White Space

White space = the gap where no brand is currently winning.

**How to find it:**
1. List the top 5–10 competitors in your category
2. Map what each claims to be (cheapest, most durable, most premium, best for beginners, etc.)
3. Look for what nobody is claiming — that's your white space
4. Validate: do customers actually care about that territory? (Read reviews)

### Two Positioning Strategies

| Strategy | What It Means | Example |
|----------|--------------|---------|
| **Category Disruption** | Prove the old way is obsolete | Netflix made Blockbuster irrelevant |
| **Category Creation** | Name a problem that didn't have a name | Airbnb created "home sharing" |

For private label at launch stage: **Category Disruption** is more practical. Find an existing category with weak players and out-position them.

### What Makes a Good Positioning Statement

Must be all three:
- **Defensible** — you can actually prove it
- **Differentiated** — meaningfully different from competitor claims
- **Resonant** — target customer hears it and thinks "yes, that's exactly what I need"

### Why This Matters for Amazon

Amazon is a search engine. Generic positioning = competing on price and PPC spend.

Specific positioning → customers searching for exactly what you offer → higher CTR → better conversion → lower effective PPC cost → faster ranking.

Consistency over 6–12 months builds a "cognitive moat" — customers start associating your brand with that position automatically. Hard to replicate once established.

## One-Line Summary

Your brand name is the first thing customers see and the thing they'll search for if they love you — it needs to be memorable, ownable, and legally clear.

## Key Takeaways

### The 6 Naming Approaches

| Type | How It Works | Examples | For Private Label |
|------|-------------|----------|------------------|
| **Descriptive** | Says exactly what it is | Grammarly, PlayStation | Easy to understand, hard to trademark |
| **Made-up word** | Invented word with no prior meaning | Kodak, Sharpie | Highly ownable, needs more marketing |
| **Alliteration** | Repeated sounds | Coca-Cola, Kit Kat | Memorable and rhythmic |
| **Intentional misspelling** | Tweak a real word | Lyft, Krispy Kreme | Avoids trademark conflicts, looks distinct |
| **Word association** | Evokes a feeling or image | Pampers, Red Bull | Emotional connection, memorable ✅ |
| **Compound/blend** | Mash two words together | Facebook, Netflix | Works well, broadly describable ✅ |

**For private label Amazon brands:** Word association and compound words tend to work best — brandable, memorable, and broad enough to expand into more products later.

### The 5-Step Naming Process

**Step 1 — Understand Your Product**
Answer these before brainstorming:
- What problem does it solve?
- Who is it for?
- What's the one feeling you want the name to evoke?

**Step 2 — Brainstorm (Generate 20–30 candidates)**

Tools:
- [Namelix.com](https://namelix.com) — AI brand name generator
- [Wordoid.com](https://wordoid.com) — invented word generator
- Thesaurus.com — find associated words
- ChatGPT — "give me 20 name ideas given [product] + [audience] + [tone]"

**Step 3 — Test With Real People**
Narrow to 5–10 names. Ask 5–10 people from your target audience:
- What does this name make you think of?
- How does it make you feel?
- Would you trust a brand with this name?
- Is it easy to spell and say?

**Step 4 — Trademark Check (Do This Before Falling in Love)**
- Search USPTO TESS database (tess.uspto.gov)
- Google for existing brands using the name
- Check domain availability
- Check social handles

**Step 5 — Finalise**
Choose the name that is:
- **Simple** — easy to say, spell, remember
- **Distinct** — nothing else like it in your category
- **Brand-aligned** — matches the feeling and positioning
- **Expandable** — won't box you in if you add products
- **Legally clear** — no trademark conflicts

### Quick Tests for a Good Name

- Can someone spell it after hearing it once? ✓
- Can someone say it after reading it once? ✓
- Does it still work if you expand to other products? ✓
- Is it available as a trademark? ✓
- Does it evoke the right feeling for your target customer? ✓

### Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Why It Hurts |
|---------|-------------|
| Skipping trademark search | Risk of legal action forcing a rebrand after launch |
| Hard to spell | Customers can't find you or search for you |
| Too generic | Blends in, hard to trademark, zero memorability |
| Too narrow | "AussieYogaMat.com" boxes you in if you expand |
| International meaning issue | Your name might mean something offensive in another language |

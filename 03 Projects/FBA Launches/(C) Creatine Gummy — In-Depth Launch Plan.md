## 3. Product Specification

### What You're Selling
Creatine monohydrate in gummy form. The market insight: creatine powder has 10,000+ review moats. Creatine gummies have 253 average reviews because the format is new. Same efficacy, better format — no mixing, no grit, portable, actually tastes good.

### Formulation Target
| Spec | Target | Notes |
|------|--------|-------|
| Active ingredient | Creatine monohydrate | Most researched form — don't use HCL or Kre-Alkalyn, they're not well-evidenced |
| Dose per serving | 3,000–5,000mg | Powder standard is 5g — gummies are limited by taste/texture at high doses. 3g is common; push manufacturer for 5g if achievable |
| Servings per container | 60 | 60 servings at ~$26 = reasonable value vs. powder |
| Flavour options | Start with 1 — watermelon, berry, or unflavored | Check what's performing in the top 10 listings. Unflavored is a gap if competitors are all flavored |
| Form | Gummy (pectin-based preferred over gelatin — vegan accessible) | Pectin = vegan-friendly. Larger addressable market. |
| Sugar content | As low as possible | Clean label selling point. Competitor weakness if they use excessive sugar/corn syrup |
| Certifications | GMP certified (mandatory), COA per batch (mandatory) | "Made in USA" if US manufacturer — put it on the label |
| Container | 60-count bottle with child-resistant lid | Standard supplement packaging — protects listing from Amazon suppression for safety reasons |

### Label Compliance (Non-Negotiable)
- Supplement Facts panel — mandatory format (serving size, servings per container, ingredient amounts, % DV)
- Manufacturer name and address
- Net quantity of contents
- Lot number and expiry date
- **Claim rule:** Structure/function claims only. "Supports muscle performance" ✅. "Builds muscle" ❌ (drug claim). "Clinically proven to increase strength" ❌ (disease/drug claim).
- Required disclaimer on every label: *"These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."*

## 4. Competitive Landscape

### What You're Up Against
The creatine gummy market is new. Top 10 organics average 253 reviews — most competitors are small brands that got in early on the format. The market isn't owned by a Goli or Nature Made yet. That's the window.

### What to Look For in Competitor Listings
Before you finalise your listing, spend 30 minutes on Amazon doing this:
1. Search "creatine gummies" — open the top 10 listings
2. For each: read the 1-star and 2-star reviews. **These are your product brief.** Common complaints = your bullet points.
3. Look at their images — are they low quality? Ugly packaging? No lifestyle shots?
4. Read their bullets — vague and generic, or specific and benefit-led?
5. Check their price and unit count — what's the $/serving?

**Likely gaps you'll find:**
- Taste complaints (too sweet, artificial aftertaste, chalky)
- Dose is lower than powder (only 3g, customers want 5g)
- No unflavored option
- Cheap-looking packaging
- Vague label claims without education about what creatine actually does

These gaps become your differentiation strategy.

### Your Positioning
Pick one lane and own it. Three options:

| Positioning | Message | Works if... |
|------------|---------|-------------|
| **Clean label** | No artificial colours, natural flavours, pectin-based | Competitors use artificial colours/sweeteners |
| **High dose** | 5,000mg per serving — full clinical dose in gummy form | Manufacturer can achieve 5g without killing taste |
| **Premium brand** | Better packaging, better brand story, better photography | You invest in creative above what competitors have done |

Recommendation: **Clean label + premium brand.** These are the most defensible and the most photogenic. High dose is ideal but depends on what the manufacturer can formulate.

### What a Good Response Looks Like
- GMP certification documentation offered without being pushed
- MOQ of 500–1,000 units (if they won't go under 2,000, move on)
- Sample cost under $300
- Lead time 6–10 weeks (longer = red flag unless justified)
- COA offered as standard
- Responsive within 48 hours (slow responses = slow production partner)

### Evaluating Samples
When samples arrive, test every one of these — document with notes and photos:

| Test | What to Check |
|------|--------------|
| Taste | Does it taste good? Artificial aftertaste? Too sweet? |
| Texture | Chewy but not gummy-bear-hard. No gritty creatine texture. |
| Smell | Should smell neutral or like the flavour. No chemical smell. |
| Label accuracy | Does the label match what was quoted (dose, ingredients)? |
| Packaging quality | Does the bottle look premium or cheap? Lid closure? |
| COA review | Does the COA match the label claim? Lab-verified creatine dose? |
| Independent lab test | Send 1 sample to LabDoor.com or Informed Sport for independent verification (~$200–400). Optional but recommended for SKU 1. |

**Don't place a production order until samples pass every test.**

### Walk-Away Criteria
Walk away from a manufacturer if:
- MOQ is over 1,500 units and they won't negotiate
- COGS is over $5.00/unit at your order quantity (kills margin at $26 selling price)
- They can't provide GMP certification
- They don't provide COA per batch
- Sample tastes bad and they say "flavour can be adjusted in production" — don't trust this without a revised sample

## 6. Unit Economics

Run this model once you have real COGS from the manufacturer. The product only proceeds if it hits 20%+ net margin at steady-state PPC spend.

### The Formula
```
Selling price:               $26.00
Amazon referral fee (15%):  -$3.90
FBA fulfillment fee:        -$4.00  (standard envelope/small standard — verify with FBA Revenue Calculator)
Inbound shipping to FBA:    -$0.60  (light supplement bottle, air freight at small volumes)
COGS:                       -$X.XX
────────────────────────────────────
Gross margin before PPC:    = $17.50 - COGS

PPC spend (% of revenue):   -$X.XX
────────────────────────────────────
Net margin:                 = $X.XX
```

### COGS Scenarios

| COGS | Gross Margin | Gross % | PPC @ 22% ($5.72) | Net Margin | Net % | Verdict |
|------|-------------|---------|-------------------|-----------|-------|---------|
| $3.00 | $14.50 | 55.8% | -$5.72 | $8.78 | 33.8% | ✅ Excellent |
| $4.50 | $13.00 | 50.0% | -$5.72 | $7.28 | 28.0% | ✅ Good |
| $5.50 | $12.00 | 46.2% | -$5.72 | $6.28 | 24.2% | ✅ Acceptable |
| $6.50 | $11.00 | 42.3% | -$5.72 | $5.28 | 20.3% | ⚠️ Minimum viable |
| $8.00 | $9.50 | 36.5% | -$5.72 | $3.78 | 14.5% | ❌ Too thin |

**Maximum viable COGS: $6.50/unit.** Above this, the margin doesn't support a real business at scale. If the manufacturer comes back above $6.50, either negotiate a higher selling price (market has room to $30+) or find a different manufacturer.

### Break-Even ACOS
```
Break-even ACOS = Gross margin / Selling price
At $4.50 COGS: $13.00 / $26.00 = 50%
```
This means any PPC sale at under 50% ACOS is profitable at the gross level. Use 50% as your ceiling during launch. Anything above 50% ACOS is buying rank at a loss — acceptable in weeks 1–4, not beyond.

### Launch Price vs. Steady-State Price
| Phase | Price | Why |
|-------|-------|-----|
| Launch (weeks 1–4) | $21.99 | Drive velocity and rank — take the margin hit |
| Growth (weeks 5–8) | $23.99 | Ease toward target |
| Steady state | $25.99–$26.99 | Market rate — maximise margin |

## 7. Amazon Account Setup

### Setup Checklist
- [ ] **Wise account** — wise.com — get US routing number + account number (takes 1–2 days)
- [ ] **Amazon Professional Seller account** — $39.99/month — use Wise US bank details when setting up
- [ ] **W-8BEN form** — complete this inside Seller Central immediately after account creation. No W-8BEN = Amazon withholds 30% of every payment
- [ ] **Seller Identity Verification (SIV)** — passport + proof of address. Have these ready.

- [ ] **Supplement category approval** — apply before listing. You need: GMP certificate from manufacturer, invoice showing product name and quantity, possibly COA. Submit via: Seller Central → Catalog → Add Products → Restricted → Request Approval

### Australia-Specific Notes
- Income is taxable in Australia, not the US (AU-US tax treaty — FBA warehousing doesn't create "permanent establishment")
- Amazon collects US sales tax — you don't handle collection. But some states (CA, TX, NY) require registration at scale. Ignore this at launch.
- Register for Australian GST once total turnover hits $75k AUD
- Talk to an accountant about whether to run this as sole trader, existing PTY, or new entity

## 9. Launch Playbook — Week by Week

### Pre-Launch (Week 10–11, before inventory arrives)
- [ ] Listing built, live, and indexed (Amazon takes 24–72 hours to index new listings — get it live before inventory arrives)
- [ ] All 7 images uploaded
- [ ] Backend keywords filled
- [ ] Supplement category approval confirmed
- [ ] PPC campaigns built but paused (Auto + Broad — ready to activate)
- [ ] "Request a Review" automation set up (Helium 10 Follow-Up or Jungle Scout emails)
- [ ] Launch price set ($21.99 — below market to drive initial velocity)
- [ ] Inventory confirmed received at FBA warehouse in Seller Central

### Week 1 — Ignition
**Goal:** Get the first 10–15 sales. Amazon's algorithm weights new products heavily in the first 1–2 weeks (the "honeymoon period"). Velocity in week 1 matters more than any other week.

- **Day 1:** Activate Auto PPC campaign. Budget: $20/day. Bidding: Dynamic Bids — Down Only.
- **Day 1:** Activate Broad Match campaign with seed keywords (see below). Budget: $15/day.
- **Day 1:** Tell friends and family the product is live — genuine purchases help velocity (do NOT offer discounts in exchange for reviews — Amazon TOS violation)
- **Day 3:** Check Auto campaign search term report — any early wins? Any obvious wasted spend? Add negatives immediately.
- **Day 7:** First weekly review (see KPIs section)

### Week 2–3 — Data Harvest
- Pull Auto campaign search term report — filter for: ≥5 clicks and at least 1 conversion
- Any keyword converting at <50% ACOS → add to Broad campaign
- Any keyword burning spend with 0 conversions after 15+ clicks → add as Negative Exact to Auto campaign
- Monitor conversion rate in Seller Central (Sessions → Orders ÷ Sessions). If <5% → listing problem, fix before scaling PPC

### Week 4 — Graduate Winners
- Pull converting keywords from Broad — move top 5–10 to **Exact Match campaign** with 25–50% higher bid
- Reduce Auto campaign budget to $12/day (let it keep discovering, but Exact is your efficiency driver now)
- Total PPC budget by week 4: ~$40–50/day across all campaigns

### Week 5–6 — Price Raise
- If BSR is improving and you have 10+ reviews → raise price to $23.99
- Continue pruning keywords weekly
- Target ACOS: start pushing toward 40–45% (tightening from launch-phase 60–70%)

### Week 8 — Steady State Price
- Raise to $25.99–$26.99 if rank is stable
- ACOS target: <35%
- TACOS target: <20% (organic sales should be starting to offset PPC spend)

### Week 12–16 — Brand Registry
- File trademark if not already done ($275 USPTO Class 5, or use IP Accelerator ~$800–1,500 via law firm for faster Brand Registry access)
- Enroll in Brand Registry once trademark application is on file
- Activate **Amazon Vine** ($200) — up to 30 Vine reviewer units. Best legitimate review acceleration available.
- Build **A+ Content** — brand story module + comparison chart. Lifts conversion 5–15%.

## 11. Review Strategy

At launch without Brand Registry, you have fewer tools. Here's what you can do:

### Request a Review Button (Seller Central)
For every order, click "Request a Review" in Seller Central. Amazon sends a generic review request email to the buyer. This is compliant. Set a reminder to do this daily or automate via Helium 10 Follow-Up / Jungle Scout emails (~$30/month as part of H10 plan you already have).

### Product Insert Card
Include a small card in every bottle. Compliant version:
> "Love your [Brand] gummies? Scan to register your purchase and leave a quick review — it means the world to a small brand."
> [QR code → Amazon review page]

Do NOT say "leave a 5-star review" or offer anything in exchange. That's against Amazon TOS and can get the listing suspended.

### Friends & Family
Tell them it exists and where to find it. If they genuinely want to buy it — great. Do NOT send them a discount code in exchange for a review. Do NOT coordinate fake reviews. Amazon catches this and the consequences are severe (account suspension).

### Amazon Vine (Week 12–16, once Brand Registry active)
- Cost: $200 per parent ASIN
- Amazon sends your product to Vine reviewers (trusted reviewers) — up to 30 units
- They leave verified reviews — typically detailed, honest, often positive for a quality product
- **Best legitimate review accelerator on Amazon.** Use it the moment Brand Registry is live.

### Review Velocity Target
| Week | Target Review Count |
|------|-------------------|
| Week 4 | 5–10 |
| Week 8 | 15–25 |
| Week 12 | 30–50 |
| Week 20 | 75+ |

If you're not hitting these numbers, the product or listing has a problem — reviews are a proxy for conversion rate and customer satisfaction.

## 14. Decision Gates

These are the points where you stop and make a go/no-go call before spending more.

### Gate 1 — After Manufacturer Quotes (Week 3–4)
**Question:** Can we get creatine gummies at ≤$6.50/unit COGS at 500–1,000 unit MOQ?
- Yes → proceed to sample order
- No → negotiate, find another manufacturer, or re-evaluate selling price

### Gate 2 — After Sample Testing (Week 5–6)
**Question:** Do the samples taste good, pass visual inspection, and have a COA that checks out?
- Yes → proceed to production order
- No → request reformulation or move to next manufacturer

### Gate 3 — After 4 Weeks Live (Week 15–16)
**Question:** Is conversion rate >5%, ACOS <60%, and at least 10 reviews at 4.0+?
- Yes → raise price, file trademark, plan SKU 2
- No → diagnose (listing? product quality? wrong keyword targeting?) before spending more on PPC

### Gate 4 — SKU 2 Decision (Month 4–5)
**Question:** Is creatine generating positive net margin after all costs?
- Yes → fund magnesium glycinate launch from creatine revenue
- No → fix creatine first, don't add complexity
